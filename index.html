How the recommended selling price is calculated

The recommended price must cover your fixed costs, fixed transaction fee, percentage-based fee, and desired profit margin.

Recommended selling price

(Base cost + Additional cost + Fixed fee) ÷ (1 − Percentage fee − Desired margin)

Percentage-based fees cannot simply be added to your fixed costs. The fee depends on the final selling price, so the formula must account for that relationship.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Profit margin measures profit as a percentage of the selling price. Markup measures profit as a percentage of cost. They are related, but they are not the same.

For example, if an item costs $80 and sells for $100, the profit is $20. The profit margin is 20%, but the markup is 25%.

Frequently asked questions

What price should I charge for a target profit margin?

Add your fixed costs and fixed transaction fee, then divide that total by one minus the percentage fee and desired margin. The KESMO Pricing Calculator performs that calculation automatically.

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Margin compares profit with the selling price. Markup compares profit with cost. A 30% markup therefore does not create a 30% profit margin.

How do transaction fees affect profit?

Fixed transaction fees add the same cost to every sale. Percentage-based fees increase as the selling price increases. Both reduce the amount you keep as profit.

What is a break-even selling price?

The break-even price is the estimated selling price that covers all entered costs and fees without producing a profit or loss.

How much can I discount without losing money?

The maximum safe discount is the difference between the recommended selling price and the break-even price. The calculator displays this difference as both a dollar amount and a percentage.

Why is a 30% markup not the same as a 30% margin?

A 30% markup is calculated from cost, while a 30% margin is calculated from the selling price. A 30% margin requires a higher selling price than a 30% markup.
